# 🎯 ReadQuest Interaction Guidelines

**Version 1.0**

---

# Why This Document Exists

This document defines **how users interact with ReadQuest**.

It does not describe UI components.

It does not describe implementation.

It defines the interaction principles that every page, every feature, every animation, every AI agent, and every user action must follow.

Every interaction should answer one question:

> **Does this make reading easier without interrupting reading?**

If the answer is no,

the interaction should be redesigned.

---

# Core Philosophy

Reading is always the primary experience.

Interaction exists only to support reading.

AI exists only to support interaction.

Everything else is secondary.

Users should feel like they are reading a book,

not operating software.

---

# Principle 1 — Reading Always Comes First

Reading is the default state.

Every feature should respect the reading flow.

The learner should never feel forced to stop reading.

Never interrupt reading with

- Popups
- Dialogs
- Full-screen overlays
- Mandatory tutorials
- Long AI explanations

The reading experience should always remain uninterrupted.

---

# Principle 2 — Progressive Disclosure

Never show everything at once.

Information should appear gradually.

Interaction hierarchy

Reading

↓

Reading Chunks

↓

Chunk Explanation

↓

Grammar

↓

Deep AI Analysis

The learner decides how much information to reveal.

Never overwhelm beginners.

Never hide useful tools from advanced learners.

---

# Principle 3 — One Interaction, One Purpose

Every interaction should have exactly one purpose.

Clicking should never open multiple unrelated actions.

Examples

Good

Click a chunk

↓

Explain that chunk

Bad

Click a chunk

↓

Grammar

↓

Translation

↓

Vocabulary

↓

Image

↓

Audio

↓

Popup

↓

Quiz

One interaction.

One value.

---

# Principle 4 — Inline Before Overlay

Whenever possible,

information should appear inside the reading experience.

Preferred

Inline explanation

↓

Floating card

↓

Side panel

Avoid

Full-screen dialog

Modal window

Page navigation

The learner should never feel removed from the book.

---

# Principle 5 — Selection Is The Universal Interaction

Selecting text is the most important interaction in ReadQuest.

Selecting text should immediately reveal a lightweight toolbar.

Available actions

Explain

Translate

Grammar

Generate Image

Listen

Save Expression

Nothing should happen automatically.

The learner remains in control.

---

# Principle 6 — Hover Should Preview

Hovering should never perform actions.

Hovering should only preview.

Examples

Hover chunk

↓

Highlight chunk

Hover dragon

↓

Small animation

Hover button

↓

Brief description

Hover should reduce uncertainty,

not trigger functionality.

---

# Principle 7 — Click Should Teach

Every click should teach something.

Examples

Click chunk

↓

Understand the chunk.

Click sentence

↓

Understand the sentence skeleton.

Click modifier

↓

Understand why it exists.

Click dragon

↓

Receive meaningful guidance.

Never make users click only to discover there was no value.

---

# Principle 8 — AI Appears Only When Valuable

AI should never interrupt.

AI should appear only when

The learner asks.

The learner hesitates.

The learner rereads repeatedly.

The learner repeatedly selects words.

The learner appears confused.

AI should never appear simply because it has something to say.

Silence is part of good interaction.

---

# Principle 9 — The Dragon Is A Companion

The dragon is never the center of attention.

The book is.

The dragon quietly observes.

The dragon occasionally helps.

The dragon celebrates.

The dragon encourages.

The dragon never lectures.

The dragon never overwhelms.

The learner should gradually trust the dragon,

not depend on it.

---

# Principle 10 — Every Success Deserves Feedback

Whenever learners genuinely understand something,

the product should acknowledge it.

Examples

Soft glow.

Small particle effect.

Dragon smiles.

Subtle sound.

Insight gained.

Reading Skill improved.

Feedback should be elegant.

Never exaggerated.

Never interrupt reading.

---

# Principle 11 — Reading Should Feel Alive

The interface should respond naturally.

Examples

Current sentence softly highlighted while reading aloud.

Current chunk highlighted during audio playback.

Chunk gently expands on hover.

Dragon reacts to learner progress.

Progress bar slowly fills.

Everything should feel alive,

never noisy.

---

# Principle 12 — Failure Should Never Feel Punishing

Learners should never feel punished.

Wrong answers are learning opportunities.

Missing one day is normal.

Changing the reading plan is encouraged.

The product adapts to the learner.

The learner should never adapt to the product.

---

# Principle 13 — Every Interaction Should Reduce Cognitive Load

Before adding an interaction,

ask

Does this simplify reading?

Does this reduce decision making?

Does this reduce memory pressure?

Does this reduce confusion?

If not,

remove it.

---

# Principle 14 — The Reading Flow

The learner should remain inside the Reading Flow.

Ideal flow

Open book

↓

Read

↓

Notice chunk

↓

Understand

↓

Continue

↓

Celebrate

↓

Continue

↓

Finish chapter

↓

Feel accomplished

Poor flow

Read

↓

Popup

↓

Grammar

↓

Close

↓

Popup

↓

Translation

↓

Close

↓

Settings

↓

Continue

Every interruption has a cost.

Protect reading flow above everything else.

---

# Principle 15 — Reading Is The Main Character

Nothing should compete with the text.

Not AI.

Not buttons.

Not colors.

Not animations.

Not the dragon.

The story should always remain the center of attention.

Everything else exists only to help the learner stay inside the story.

---

# Principle 16 — Micro Interactions

Micro interactions should create delight,

not distraction.

Good examples

Hover animation

Button ripple

Chunk highlight

Dragon blink

Soft page transition

Gentle success animation

Bad examples

Large popups

Screen shaking

Confetti explosions

Excessive motion

Multiple simultaneous animations

---

# Principle 17 — Context-Aware Interaction

Every interaction should understand context.

The dragon knows

Current page.

Current sentence.

Current chunk.

Reading progress.

Current reading mode.

Reading history.

Recent difficulties.

AI responses should always be contextual.

Never generic.

---

# Principle 18 — User Control

The learner controls the experience.

Users decide

How much AI appears.

Whether grammar is visible.

Whether translation is visible.

Reading speed.

Chunk mode.

Theme.

Font.

The learner should always feel

"I am reading the book."

Never

"The software is controlling me."

---

# Principle 19 — Delight Through Discovery

ReadQuest should surprise learners,

but never interrupt them.

Examples

The dragon quietly congratulates the learner after understanding a difficult sentence.

A new Reading Skill appears after mastering a Quest.

The map expands after finishing a chapter.

A hidden animation appears after a reading streak.

Discovery creates emotional attachment.

---

# Principle 20 — The Ultimate Interaction Goal

Every interaction should move the learner toward one feeling.

> "Wait...

> I can actually read this."

If an interaction does not strengthen this feeling,

it should not exist in ReadQuest.
